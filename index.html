Learn Economics with Phoebe

Economics is the study of how people respond to prices, incentives and scarcity, and a data team meets all three every week without calling them that. These sixteen sessions teach the fundamentals twice: once as ideas anyone can use, with no code, and once as numbers you can compute, on a real marketplace. The running finding is the same in both: on 203 products that repriced often enough to be read, the same 2,216 rows give a price elasticity of +0.008 when products are compared with each other, -0.108 when they are pooled, and -1.788 when each is compared with itself. A first analysis reports the second number. A pricing decision needs the third. The bench in the last session refits all of them in your browser, and the lever everyone reaches for gets the sign of the revenue decision wrong.

Foundations track 🧭 · six sessions, no code

For anyone who prices anything, argues for a budget, or reads a number a model produced. Prices as information, incentives and elasticity, marginal thinking and opportunity cost, when markets fail, money, inflation and interest, and games and moats. The marketplace's real counts appear on every page; the code does not.

🏷️ Foundations session 1 · start here

Prices are information

Supply and demand as a description of where a price settles, not who sets it. A price as a message carrying knowledge nobody holds in full. Along the curve, or the curve moved. Of 32,216 real products, 203 moved price often enough to say anything about response.

How much people respond. Elastic versus inelastic, the revenue rule, who really pays a tax, and the Olist number read as an elasticity: a range, -1.5 to -2.2, and why a range is the honest shape.

Decisions at the margin, sunk costs and the fork, the next best use, comparative advantage, and why a physical unit, a copy of software and one more prediction have three different marginal costs.
